Best Free Invoice Tracking Templates for Google Sheets (2026)

You Don't Need to Build Your Invoice Tracker from Scratch

If you use Google Sheets to track invoices, you have probably spent time setting up columns, formatting cells, and writing formulas to calculate totals and overdue amounts. It works, but it takes a while to get right. The good news is that you do not have to start from a blank spreadsheet. There are excellent free invoice tracking templates that give you a ready-made structure with all the columns, formulas, and formatting already in place. You just open the template, make a copy, and start entering your data.

But here is the real question that most template articles skip over: once you have a beautifully structured invoice tracking spreadsheet, how do you actually fill it with data efficiently? The template solves the structure problem. It does not solve the data entry problem. We will get to that later. First, let us look at the five best free invoice tracking templates for Google Sheets in 2026 and help you pick the one that fits your workflow.

Top 5 Free Invoice Tracking Templates for Google Sheets

1. Smartsheet Invoice Tracker

Smartsheet offers a well-designed, downloadable Google Sheets template that covers all the basics a freelancer or small business owner needs. It tracks invoice number, client name, amount due, date sent, due date, payment status, and amount paid. The layout is clean, easy to follow, and does not require any technical knowledge to use. You download the file, open it in Google Sheets, and start entering your invoices right away.

This template is a great fit for freelancers who work with a handful of clients and want a simple, no-fuss way to keep track of what has been invoiced and what has been paid. It does not try to do too much, which is a strength. If you just need a straightforward invoice tracking spreadsheet that works, this is a solid choice.

Download from Smartsheet

2. Coefficient Invoice Tracking Template

Coefficient takes the invoice tracking template a step further by offering built-in connections to live data sources. Their free Google Sheets template tracks payment status, outstanding amounts, and includes aging analysis so you can see at a glance which invoices are 30, 60, or 90 days overdue. The calculations update automatically as you add new data, which saves you from having to build and maintain your own formulas.

This template is best suited for small businesses that want more than just a list of invoices. If you need to track payment aging, monitor outstanding balances, and get a clearer picture of your cash flow, Coefficient gives you that out of the box. The learning curve is minimal, and the automated calculations make it feel more like a lightweight accounting tool than a basic spreadsheet.

Download from Coefficient

3. Xappex Invoice Tracker

Xappex offers a fully customizable invoice tracker template with a clean, minimal layout. It tracks the essentials: invoice number, client, amount, date, and payment status, with clear visual indicators for paid, pending, and overdue invoices. The template is designed to be easy to modify, so if you want to add extra columns for project names, purchase order numbers, or notes, you can do that without breaking anything.

This template is ideal for people who value simplicity and want a clean starting point they can make their own. If you have tried other templates that felt cluttered or overloaded with features you do not need, Xappex is a refreshing alternative. It gives you just enough structure without getting in your way.

Download from Xappex

4. FreshBooks Google Sheets Template

FreshBooks is one of the most well-known names in invoicing, and they offer a free Google Sheets invoice template that reflects their professional design standards. The layout is polished and client-facing, which means you can use it not just for internal tracking but also for generating invoices that look good when sent to clients. It includes fields for your business details, the client's information, line items, tax calculations, and payment terms.

This template is the best choice for freelancers who need to both track and send invoices. If you want a single spreadsheet that serves as your invoice tracker and your invoice generator, FreshBooks gives you a template that looks professional enough to email directly to clients. It is especially useful if you are not ready to pay for invoicing software but still want your invoices to look polished.

Download from FreshBooks

5. Google Sheets Built-in Template

You might not even need to leave Google Sheets to find a template. Google has a built-in Template Gallery that includes invoice-related templates. To access it, open Google Sheets, click on "Template Gallery" at the top of the home screen, and browse the available options. The built-in templates are basic but functional, and the biggest advantage is that there is nothing to download or import. You just click and start working.

This template is the right pick if you want to get started in 30 seconds with zero friction. It will not have the advanced features of the other templates on this list, but it gives you a clean starting point that lives entirely within Google Sheets. If your invoice tracking needs are simple and you just want something that works immediately, this is the fastest way to begin.

Open Google Sheets and go to Template Gallery to find it.

The Gap That Templates Don't Fill

All five of these templates will give you a well-organized invoice tracking spreadsheet. They handle the structure, the formatting, and often the formulas too. But there is one thing none of them solve: the actual data entry. Every invoice that arrives in your email still needs to be opened, read through, and then manually typed into the spreadsheet. If you receive 10 invoices a week, that is 10 times you need to find the invoice number, the date, the vendor name, the total, and the due date, and type each one into the right cells.

This is the gap between having a great template and having a great workflow. The template is the container. But filling that container still takes time and effort, and it is exactly the kind of repetitive work where mistakes happen. You misread a number, you skip a row, you type the wrong date. These small errors add up, especially at the end of the month when you are trying to reconcile everything. A good template deserves a good way to populate it.

How to Choose the Right Template

With five solid options on the table, how do you decide which one to use? Here are the key things to consider:

  • How many invoices do you handle per month? If you deal with just a handful, a simple template like Smartsheet or the Google Sheets built-in will work fine. If you process dozens, you will benefit from the automated calculations in Coefficient's template.
  • Do you need aging analysis? Aging analysis shows you how long invoices have been outstanding, broken down by 30, 60, and 90 days. Coefficient's template includes this. Others do not, though you could add it manually.
  • Are you tracking invoices you receive or invoices you send? Most templates on this list are designed for tracking invoices you receive from vendors. If you also need to create and send invoices to clients, the FreshBooks template is designed with that in mind.
  • Do you need multi-currency support? If you work with international clients or vendors, check whether the template handles multiple currencies. Most free templates assume a single currency, so you may need to add a currency column yourself.
  • How important is customization? If you want to add your own columns, change the layout, or integrate with other tools, go with a template that is designed to be flexible, like Xappex. If you just want something that works out of the box, the Google Sheets built-in template is the fastest starting point.
  • Do you want to automate data entry later? If automation is on your radar, pick a template with a clear, consistent column structure. This makes it much easier to connect tools like AutoInv down the road.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I customize these templates?

Yes, all of them. Since they are Google Sheets files, you can add columns, change formatting, insert formulas, and adjust the layout however you like. Some templates, like Xappex, are specifically designed to be easy to modify. Just make sure to work on a copy of the original so you always have a clean version to go back to.

Do these templates work on mobile?

Yes. Google Sheets works on both Android and iOS through the Google Sheets app. All of these templates will open and function on your phone or tablet. That said, editing a spreadsheet on a small screen is never as comfortable as on a computer, especially with wider templates that have many columns. For quick lookups and status checks, mobile works great. For heavy data entry, you will probably want a laptop.

How do I track overdue invoices?

Most of these templates include a status column where you can mark invoices as paid, pending, or overdue. Some, like Coefficient's template, go further by automatically calculating how many days an invoice is past due. If your chosen template does not include overdue tracking, you can add a simple formula that compares the due date to today's date and flags anything that is past due. A basic formula like =IF(due_date < TODAY(), "Overdue", "On time") will do the job.

Can I automate the data entry part?

Yes. Tools like AutoInv are designed specifically for this. AutoInv is a free Google Sheets add-on that uses AI to extract invoice data from text you paste into the spreadsheet. It pulls out the invoice number, date, vendor name, total amount, and due date automatically. If you upgrade to Pro, it can also import invoices directly from Gmail. It pairs well with any of the templates listed above.
